Friday Executive Security Brief · 13 November 2026

Security evidence is becoming standardized at every boundary.

This week’s strongest releases sharpen the language for adversarial AI, simplify national incident reporting, and strengthen cryptographic evidence for internet routing and industrial systems.

NIST SP 2400-102e2026: Adversarial Machine Learning Taxonomy

National Institute of Standards and Technology · Initial public draft, 9 November 2026 · NIST draft

What is genuinely new: NIST extends its adversarial-machine-learning taxonomy to agentic, highly capable systems. It organizes attacks by lifecycle stage, attacker goal, capability and knowledge, and adds an agentic-attack index for fine-grained reference.

Why it matters: Agencies, suppliers and assessors cannot compare controls or incidents when terms such as poisoning, evasion, misuse, prompt injection and agent compromise are used inconsistently.

Practical implication: Adopt the taxonomy in threat models, test plans, incident categories and procurement requirements. Map each relevant attack class to prevention, detection, containment, evidence and recovery controls; submit operational comments by 18 December.

Enhanced Cyber-Incident Reporting Form

Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency · 9 November 2026 · CISA incident reporting

What is genuinely new: CISA introduced an enhanced intake that supports structured initial reporting and subsequent updates as facts develop.

Why it matters: Incident teams often lose time reformatting the same evidence for executives, regulators, insurers, law enforcement and sector partners. A clearer national intake model reinforces the value of one authoritative incident record.

Practical implication: Align playbooks and case-management schemas with CISA’s fields. Capture the detection source, affected mission, identities, assets, timeline, observed effects, containment, evidence status and responsible contacts once, then generate audience-specific reports.

Autonomous System Provider Authorization Profile — Revision 31

IETF SIDROPS Working Group · Standards-track draft, 10 November 2026 · IETF draft

What is genuinely new: The mature revision specifies the signed RPKI object through which an autonomous system authorizes its transit providers, enabling detection and mitigation of route leaks.

Why it matters: Zero trust inside an agency does not protect services when internet routes are misdirected upstream. Routing integrity is part of the end-to-end trust boundary for cloud, public portals and emergency communications.

Practical implication: Ask network and hosting providers about RPKI route-origin validation, ASPA readiness, monitoring and response. Include routing-security evidence in cloud and connectivity procurements.

RPKI-to-Router Protocol Version 2 — Revision 29

IETF SIDROPS Working Group · 12 November 2026 · IETF draft

What is genuinely new: The update advances the protocol used to deliver validated RPKI payloads to routers, with active cross-area security and operational review.

Why it matters: Signed routing objects have little value if validation results cannot be delivered to enforcement points reliably and observed during failure.

Practical implication: Treat RPKI cache-to-router delivery as production security infrastructure. Require redundancy, freshness monitoring, fail-state policy, configuration testing and logging that distinguishes invalid routes from unavailable validation data.

Siemens Industrial Edge, Building Automation and Grid Products Advisories

CISA Industrial Control Systems · 12 November 2026 · CISA ICS advisories

What is genuinely new: CISA released a coordinated set of advisories affecting industrial-edge management, building automation, gridscale and ruggedized communications products.

Why it matters: Public facilities and utilities frequently operate these platforms across IT, OT, energy and vendor-maintained boundaries where ownership and patch authority are fragmented.

Practical implication: Reconcile advisories against exact product, firmware and deployment role; restrict management access; validate backups and safe restart procedures; and require vendors to document remediation and residual risk.

Executive takeaway

  • Use a shared adversarial-AI language. Taxonomy should connect threat models, testing, incidents and procurement.
  • Capture incident evidence once. One authoritative record should support operations, leadership and external reporting.
  • Extend zero trust to infrastructure dependencies. Routing validation and OT management planes are part of the mission boundary.

Emerging pattern: Assurance is moving toward standardized, machine-verifiable evidence at every handoff—from AI lifecycle risk and incident intake to internet routes, validation caches and industrial control planes.
